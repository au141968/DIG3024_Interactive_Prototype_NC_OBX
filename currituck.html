A Need for a Light in Corolla

Built in 1873, the Currituck Lighthouse is operated by a private organization. Interior view looking upward. Many shipwrecks occured on the shoals nearby which prompted Congress to commission the lighthouse. The most powerful Fresnel lens built, a 1st order, enabled the light to reach 18 miles out to sea. The windows were positioned to face the shore in the lighthouse's tower. Along with the view from the top, this enabled the keepers to watch incoming ships. If any were in trouble, they could alert Currituck Beach Life Saving Station.

The Precursor of the U.S. Coast Guard

The Outer Banks was called the Graveyard of the Atlantic due to the number of shipwrecks and dangerous conditions there. The lighthouses were aids to navigation but if a ship was in distress, help was needed. The U.S. Lifesaving Service was formed in 1878. This service would attempt to rescue victims in a shipwreck. It was originally made up of volunteers. The need was high and Congress approved more of these lifesaving stations to be built along the Outer Banks. These would be staffed year round and included lookout towers. This was part of the original rope and pulley system that could launch lifesaving boats into the ocean faster than by carrying them. This enabled the men to get to the shipwreck faster to save more lives. In 1915, the U.S. Lifesaving Service became the U.S. Coast Guard.
