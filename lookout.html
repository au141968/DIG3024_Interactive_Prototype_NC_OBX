A Beacon Built In The Wilderness With A Unique Diamond Pattern

The wild horses of Shackleford Banks near Cape Lookout. It is thought that their ancestors were horses from Spanish shipwrecks. The strategic location of Cape Lookout lighthouse enabled a wide view of the surrounding coastline which was helpful in World War II for spotting ships and unfortunately, submarines. Originally, there was a large 1st order Fresnel Lens that sat in the lantern room. It was removed when the Cape Lookout Lighthouse was automated. This lighthouse can be reached by ferry, and visitors can climb up to the top on certain days. While looking out over the ocean, one can imagine what lurked below the surface several decades ago.

Torpedo Junction

During World War II, the waters off the Outer Banks were invaded by a deadly new threat, German U-boats. Known as Operation Drumbeat, U-boats sank almost 100 ships. The East Coast was unprotected and vulnerable to these attacks in 1942. Radio equipment, like these, were used in radio rooms to alert U.S. forces when a U-boat was spotted. Many civilians patroled the beaches to serve as lookouts as well. The Civil Air Patrol flew private small planes to hunt for submarines. A cemetary for some of the men from the HMT Bedfordshire. This British trawler was fitted with guns and loaned to the U.S. to patrol shipping lanes. It was torpedoed in May 1942 with the loss of all 37 men.
